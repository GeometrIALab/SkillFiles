# Licences

The pack uses three licences, one for each kind of file, so that each can be used the
way it is meant to be used.

| What | Files | Licence | SPDX |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instructions and documentation | `README.md`, `LICENSE.md`, every `SKILL.md`, everything in `references/` | [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt) | `CC-BY-4.0` |
| Scripts | `package.sh`, everything in `scripts/` | [MIT License](LICENSES/MIT.txt) | `MIT` |
| Templates | everything in a `templates/` folder, including `templates/field-pack/` | [CC0 1.0 Universal](LICENSES/CC0-1.0.txt) (public domain dedication) | `CC0-1.0` |

## What this means in practice

- **Using the skills needs no attribution.** Installing them, letting an agent read
  them and following them in your own research does not trigger any licence condition.
  The CC BY condition applies when you **share** the skill text itself or an adaptation of it.
- **Sharing or adapting the instructions (CC BY 4.0).** You may copy, redistribute,
  translate and adapt them for any purpose, including commercially. When you share them,
  credit the source, link to the licence and say whether you made changes. Suggested
  credit:

  > "Mathematical research skills for AI agents" by GeometrIA Lab, CBG, licensed under
  > CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), with a link to where
  > you obtained it. Note any changes you made.

- **Scripts (MIT).** Use, modify and redistribute them freely. Keep the copyright and
  permission notice with copies of the scripts.
- **Templates (CC0).** The templates are made to be copied into your own projects,
  notes and manuscripts, such as `PROJECT_SUMMARY.md`, `PROOF_PROGRESS.md`, the LaTeX paper
  skeleton and the run manifest. They are dedicated to the public domain, so filled-in
  project files and papers carry no attribution requirement.

## Skills distributed on their own

Each skill folder contains a `LICENSE.txt` with the same terms and the full MIT text,
so a skill that is copied or zipped on its own (for example by `package.sh`) still
carries its licence.

The full licence texts are in [`LICENSES/`](LICENSES/). This summary is for
convenience only. Where it differs from the licence texts, the licence texts govern.
